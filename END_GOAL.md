# End Goal: Full Simulation of the CEA VKS Experiment

## 1. Introduction to the VKS Experiment

The **VKS experiment** (Von Kármán Sodium) is a large-scale experimental device
built by CEA-DAM and CNRS to study the dynamo effect generated by the turbulent
magnetic convection of liquid sodium. The experiment consists of two counter-
rotating impellers immersed in a spherical shell filled with sodium, which
generate a strong, turbulent, rotating flow. By measuring the magnetic field
produced by the flowing sodium, the experiment aims to validate dynamo theory
and understand the onset of self-sustained magnetic fields in planetary cores
and astrophysical objects.

The VKS experiment is unique because it accesses the high-Rm (magnetic
Reynolds number) regime relevant to astrophysical and geophysical dynamos, while
being fully controllable in the laboratory. The two impellers can be rotated at
different speeds and in different directions, allowing the study of various flow
configurations such as co-rotation, counter-rotation, and mixed scenarios.

Key scientific goals of the VKS experiment:
- Observe the transition from decaying magnetic fields to dynamo action
- Measure the critical magnetic Reynolds number for dynamo onset
- Study the statistical properties of the generated magnetic field
- Validate theoretical models of dynamo threshold and saturation
- Access parameter ranges not reachable in numerical simulations alone

The experiment typically operates with:
- Liquid sodium as the conducting fluid (high magnetic permeability, high
  magnetic diffusivity)
- Two impellers of different sizes and speeds
- Diameter ~ 1 m, height ~ 0.5 m
- Flow velocities up to several m/s
- Magnetic Reynolds numbers up to Rm ~ 1000
- Measurement arrays of induction coils and magnetic probes

## 2. Mathematical Models for VKS

To simulate the VKS experiment, several coupled physical equations must be
solved. The core equations are:

### 2.1 Magneto-hydrodynamics (MHD) Equations

The fundamental equations governing the VKS experiment are the MHD equations:

**Continuity equation (incompressibility):**
```
\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0
```

**Momentum equation (Navier-Stokes with Lorentz force):**
```
\rho \left( \frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + \mathbf{u} \cdot \nabla \mathbf{u} \right)
= -\nabla p + \mu \nabla^2 \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{J} \times \mathbf{B}
```
where `\mathbf{J} = \nabla \times \mathbf{B} / \mu_0` is the current density.

**Induction equation (magnetic field evolution):**
```
\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t} = \nabla \times (\mathbf{u} \times \mathbf{B})
+ \eta \nabla^2 \mathbf{B}
```
where `\eta = 1/(\mu_0 \sigma)` is the magnetic diffusivity, `\sigma` being the
electrical conductivity.

**Solenoidal constraint:**
```
\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0
```

### 2.2 Turbulence Models

Since the VKS flow is fully turbulent, closure models are required:

**k-epsilon model:**
- Transport equations for turbulent kinetic energy `k` and dissipation rate `\epsilon`
- Modified production terms to account for rotation and magnetic fields

**Large Eddy Simulation (LES):**
- Filtered Navier-Stokes equations with subgrid-scale model
- Smagorinsky-Lilly model or dynamic mixed model for the subgrid stress

**Reynolds Stress Models:**
- Transport equations for the Reynolds stress tensor
- Better capture of anisotropic turbulence in rotating flows

### 2.3 Dimensionless Parameters

- **Magnetic Reynolds number:** `Rm = UL/\eta` (advection vs. diffusion)
- **Reynolds number:** `Re = UL/\nu` (inertial vs. viscous forces)
- **Interaction parameter:** `N = \sigma B^2 L / (\rho U)` (Lorentz vs. inertial forces)
- **Prandtl number:** `Pm = \nu / \eta` (momentum vs. magnetic diffusion)
- **Rossby number:** `Ro = U / (2\Omega L)` (inertial vs. Coriolis forces)

For the VKS experiment, typical values are Rm ~ 100-1000, Re ~ 10^5-10^6,
Pm ~ 10^-5 (for sodium), making it high-Rm but low-Pm dynamo.

## 3. Implementation with TRUST, trust-LiMe, and TrioCFD

### 3.1 TRUST (CEA's native simulation platform)

TRUST is the primary simulation platform used at CEA for MHD and fluid dynamics.
Implementation approach:

**Discretization:**
- PolyMAC (Polyhedral Mesh Adaptive Configuration) finite volume method
- Face-based and cell-based discretization for velocity, pressure, and magnetic fields
- Support for unstructured meshes matching the spherical geometry of VKS

**MHD Implementation in TRUST:**
- The MHD equations are implemented through the `Pb_MHD` problem type
- Induction equation solved using convection-diffusion framework
- Lorentz force `\mathbf{J} \times \mathbf{B}` computed from current density
- Boundary conditions: insulating or perfectly conducting walls
- Time-stepping: implicit Euler or Crank-Nicolson for diffusion, Adams-Bashforth for convection

**Turbulence in TRUST:**
- Built-in `Navier_Stokes_standard` equation with various turbulence closures
- `k-epsilon` model implemented through source terms
- LES possible through `Schema_LES` and subgrid-scale models
- User-defined source terms via `Terme_Source` for custom MHD terms
- *Note: the magnetic induction equation in trust-LiMe assumes molecular magnetic diffusivity η only (no turbulent EDM); see turbulence section of END_GOAL.md §2.2 and AGENTS.md "Known gaps" for the deferral rationale.*
**VKS-specific TRUST features:**
- Spherical geometry handling through polyhedral meshes
- Two impeller rotation implemented through moving mesh or body force
- Sodium physical properties (conductivity, density, viscosity)
- Impeller speed control as time-dependent boundary conditions
- Magnetic probe integration for field measurement simulation

### 3.2 trust-LiMe (This Module)

trust-LiMe adds the magnetic induction equation on top of the TRUST kernel.
Implementation approach:

**Core capabilities:**
- Standalone magnetic induction equation (M0/M1 mode)
- Coupled mode with optional Navier-Stokes equation (Lorentz force)
- Post-treatment fields: cyclotron pulsation, magnetic energy, Poynting vector,
  helicity, current density, magnetic tension
- Green-Gauss reconstruction of `\nabla \mathbf{B}` for divergence checking

**MHD Implementation in trust-LiMe:**
- `Convection_Diffusion_Magnetic_field` equation class
- `Pb_MHD` problem extension with optional `Navier_Stokes_standard`
- `force_lorentz` keyword for enabling J\times B coupling
- `induction_stretching` source term for M1 effects
- Velocity field from `vitesse_magnetique` (standalone) or
  `vitesse_hydraulique` (coupled mode)

**Trust-LiMe + VKS:**
- The module solves the standalone induction equation **and**, in coupled mode,
  the two-equation low-Rm MHD system (magnetic + Navier-Stokes, with both
  feedback directions live: `force_lorentz` on momentum, `vitesse_hydraulique`
  on the magnetic transport velocity)
- For full VKS, would need: the high-Rm nonlinear regime — turbulent EDM,
  a fully nonlinear (non-lagged) coupling, and unsteady dynamo saturation
- Impeller rotation through time-dependent velocity field
- Sodium properties parameterization
- Parallel execution for large 3D meshes
- Post-treatment of dynamo-generated fields

**trust-LiMe limitations for VKS:**
- Low-Rm approximation (Rm ≪ 1) validated against breeding blanket ranges
- Two-way coupling is implemented but **lagged and explicit**: the staggered
  scheme `bˢ⁺¹ = F(bˢ,uˢ)`, `uˢ⁺¹ = G(uˢ,bˢ)` linearizes both the Lorentz and the
  stretching terms — not the nonlinear high-Rm MHD a dynamo needs
- Linearized convective term `(u·\nabla)b - (b·\nabla)u - \eta\Delta b = 0`
- Turbulent magnetic diffusivity is now available on three paths: a
  prescribed field, an explicit k/epsilon pair, and (in coupled mode) the
  hydraulic k-ε model's own k and ε; an independent magnetic k-ε closure
  (k_b, epsilon_b) is implemented in source but not yet validated.
- No MHD modifications to the hydraulic turbulence model (Tassone et al.
  §4) and no high-Rm dynamo saturation are implemented yet.
- Div-b not enforced (assumed but not proven globally)

### 3.3 TrioCFD (Turbulence CFD Solver)

TrioCFD is an open-source CFD solver capable of MHD simulations.
Implementation approach:

**TrioCFD overview:**
- Finite volume code developed at CEA-DAM
- Parallel (MPI) execution
- Support for Newtonian and non-Newtonian fluids
- MHD capabilities through user-defined functions

**MHD in TrioCFD:**
- Solves the same MHD equation set as TRUST
- Uses Nedelec edge elements or finite volumes for magnetic field
- Lorentz force computation through user kernels
- Support for various conductivity profiles
- Parallel domain decomposition for large-scale runs

**Turbulence models in TrioCFD:**
- Standard k-epsilon model
- RNG k-epsilon model
- Realizable k-epsilon model
- LES with Smagorinsky, WALE, or dynamic models
- DNS capabilities for reduced-dimensional cases

**TrioCFD + VKS:**
- Well-suited for high-Pm number dynamo simulations
- Parallel scalability for full spherical geometry
- Comparison platform with TRUST results
- Turbulence model sensitivity studies
- Integration with experimental data assimilation

## 4. Comparison and Synergy

| Feature | TRUST | trust-LiMe | TrioCFD |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MHD equations** | Full implementation | Standalone + coupled mode | Full implementation |
| **Turbulence models** | k-epsilon, LES | Basic, no turbulent MHD | k-epsilon variants, LES |
| **Geometry** | General polyhedral | Spherical preferred | General unstructured |
| **Parallel scalability** | Excellent (MPI) | Good (sequential default) | Excellent (MPI) |
| **VKS suitability** | Excellent (native) | Good (needs extensions) | Excellent (native MHD) |
| **Post-treatment** | Comprehensive | Rich (MHD-specific fields) | Standard CFD |
| **Low-Rm validity** | Validated | Validated | Partial |

**Synergy strategy:**
1. **trust-LiMe** provides the magnetic foundation and post-treatment
2. **TRUST** handles the full Navier-Stokes + MHD coupling
3. **TrioCFD** offers alternative turbulence models and parallel scalability
4. **Experimental data** from VKS validates all three codes
5. **Parameter scanning** across Rm, Re, Pm using different codes

## 5. Roadmap for VKS Simulation

### Phase 1: Code Preparation (0-6 months)
- Extend trust-LiMe to full two-equation coupled mode — **done** (low-Rm,
  lagged: `force_lorentz` on momentum + `vitesse_hydraulique` on the magnetic
  transport velocity)
- Implement impeller rotation boundary conditions
- Sodium property parameterization (temperature-dependent conductivity)
- TrioCFD MHD capability assessment
- Mesh generation for spherical shell with impellers

### Phase 2: Baseline Simulations (6-12 months)
- Decaying field simulations (no dynamo, Rm < Rm_crit)
- Validate against VKS startup phase
- Mesh convergence study
- Turbulence model sensitivity

### Phase 3: Dynamo Threshold (12-18 months)
- Rm scan across critical value
- Identify onset of magnetic field growth
- Compare threshold across codes
- Validate against experimental critical Rm

### Phase 4: Fully Developed Dynamo (18-24 months)
- Saturated state simulation
- Statistical properties of magnetic field
- Comparison with VKS measurements
- High-Pm regime extension

### Phase 5: Integration and Publication (24-30 months)
- Integrated report combining all three codes
- Peer-reviewed publication results
- Open-source code releases
- Database of VKS simulation data

## 6. Conclusion

The full simulation of the CEA VKS experiment represents a significant
computational challenge at the intersection of MHD, turbulence, and dynamo theory.
By leveraging the complementary strengths of TRUST (mature MHD implementation),
trust-LiMe (magnetic post-treatment and low-Rm modeling), and TrioCFD (turbulence
models and parallel scalability), the CEA team can build a comprehensive
simulation suite that not only reproduces but also predicts VKS phenomena.

The key challenge is extending the low-Rm validated trust-LiMe mode to the
high-Rm dynamo regime, and ensuring consistency across the three simulation
platforms. The experimental data from VKS provides the ultimate validation
ground, enabling code development tuned to real-world parameters rather than
idealized test cases.

This end-to-end simulation capability will not only advance our understanding
of the VKS experiment but also provide a template for simulating other dynamo
systems, from planetary cores to astrophysical jets.
